The 60-Second Version
  • The 2008 financial crisis wasn't a failure of the system—it was the system working as designed, cycling wealth upward through deliberate credit expansion and contraction.

  • Marpole.AI is Dmitri's attempt to build a parallel system using AI and blockchain to connect, inform, and coordinate people outside the incumbent power structure.

  • For entrepreneurs, the immediate priorities are reducing fixed expense exposure, staying debt-aware, adapting to AI-driven change, and making decisions from a clear, rested mind.
About the Guest

Dmitri Maxim

After losing everything in the 2008 financial crisis, he spent 17 years applying forensic accounting discipline to the global financial system. The Conversation

What We Covered

01 · The Origin Story
Take us through that journey — what happened to you, what shocked you, and how did it lead to 17 years of investigation?
  • In 2008, Dmitri pooled all his resources into what he expected to be a life-changing investment and lost everything when the financial crisis hit.
  • At a court hearing — joining by phone — he argued that large corporations had taken his money and others' through manipulative practices. The judge told him it was all done lawfully. That single word launched the investigation.
  • He spent nearly 17 years moving across disciplines — finance, cognitive science, psychology, hypnosis, political structure, and history — trying to understand why some people consistently lose while others consistently win.
  • His conclusion: the system is not broken. It is performing exactly as designed — just not for ordinary people. The mechanism is extraction, not malfunction.
  • That conclusion led him to reverse-engineer the system and build Marpole.AI — using the same structural logic the system uses, but redirecting the information and benefit flows back toward individuals.
02 · The Breakthrough
You said the system is designed around whoever controls the counting. What was the core insight that made you think you could actually build something that worked against that?
  • The central insight came from forensic accounting: whoever controls the measurement controls the outcome. Official indicators diverge from lived reality because the people setting the metrics are the same people benefiting from the gap.
  • Dmitri identified a structural asymmetry — those in power are information-connected, money-connected, and decision-connected. Everyone else operates in isolated bubbles and competes against each other rather than cooperating.
  • The GameStop episode in 2021 proved the model in reverse: retail investors on Reddit organized without a central leader, collectively bought a shorted stock, and burned roughly $10 billion in hedge fund positions before the buy button was simply switched off.
  • That episode confirmed his thesis — collective, coordinated action works, but it collapses without a permanent trust layer underneath it. The hedge funds won because they could coordinate; the retail buyers could not sustain it.
  • The breakthrough was recognizing that blockchain could serve as that trust layer — a tamper-proof ledger of collective decisions and interactions that no single authority can retroactively alter.
03 · The Growth Engine
How are you actually building Marpole — what does the product look like, and how does it reach people?
  • The first product is a free AI tool on Marpole.ai called the Audit of Words — an algorithm that identifies the single words or phrases quietly driving a person's financial and personal decisions, then helps them reframe those patterns.
  • The second product in development is Marpole Scanning — a smartphone app that lets users scan any headline, marketing slogan, loan offer, or financial label and surfaces the real numbers behind it, such as the total interest paid over a 30-year mortgage versus the monthly payment figure advertised.
  • Blockchain is being built into the platform to record all user interactions and collective decisions in a way that cannot be revised — creating an environment of verifiable trust across a distributed user base.
  • Dmitri draws a direct parallel to his smart glass manufacturing company, where an AI system he built several years ago eliminated approximately 10,000 hours of repetitive employee work — handling inbound calls, routing, email follow-up, SMS, and appointment scheduling automatically.
  • The book — 444 pages, also available as a free audio download — serves as the primary distribution engine, covering 5,000 years of historical evidence for how power structures have operated and what Dmitri predicts happens by 2030 if no alternative system is in place.
04 · The Filter
When you look at a business or a financial position — your own or someone else's — what tells you to walk away or stay out?
  • Dmitri separates every cost structure into variable and fixed before making any commitment — variable expenses can be scaled down during turbulence, but a fixed obligation like a five-year, $50,000-per-month lease cannot be walked back regardless of revenue.
  • He stress-tests asset positions against a 40% decline scenario and asks whether debt obligations would survive that compression — specifically whether loan covenants require covering the gap between reduced asset value and the outstanding loan balance.
  • He will not build a plan with a single scenario. Any serious decision requires a plan B, C, and D mapped out in advance, with explicit answers to how long a distressed position could be held and what refinancing options exist at that point.
  • Any business whose value proposition depends on conditions staying stable is flagged — he uses the example of a premium leather whip manufacturer wiped out by the automobile, and applies the same test to current businesses facing AI-driven displacement.
  • He treats any situation where his information or time advantage is zero as high risk — if he cannot independently verify what is being presented to him, he assumes the presented number is not the real number, and declines until he can audit it himself.
05 · The People
You built an AI system inside your smart glass manufacturing company — what does that actually look like when a customer interacts with it, and what did it replace?
  • Before the AI system, Dmitri's smart glass company relied on employees to handle inbound calls, answer product questions, route inquiries, send follow-up emails, write SMS messages, and schedule appointments — all manually, all repetitive.
  • The AI system he built several years ago now handles every one of those touchpoints automatically, freeing roughly 10,000 hours of employee time that previously went to rote tasks.
  • Dmitri trains his sales people around a core insight: customers almost never state their real need directly. They give vague signals because they fear being taken advantage of the moment they reveal urgency — his example is someone who needs a couch in two hours before a family visit but won't say so because the salesperson would stop discounting.
  • The job of anyone customer-facing, human or AI, is to keep asking until the underlying pressure surfaces — what is the real deadline, the real fear, the real constraint — because that is the only way to actually solve the problem rather than just answer the surface question.
  • He applies the same principle internally with employees, arguing that most workplace friction comes from managers projecting their own interpretation onto a situation rather than finding out what is actually driving the behavior.
06 · What Broke
You mentioned that big companies doing mergers and acquisitions typically build only one scenario plan — what breaks when that single plan hits reality, and what should replace it?
  • The specific thing that breaks is the single-scenario financial plan — Dmitri reviewed M&A management processes and found that even sophisticated organizations routinely enter major commitments with only one projected outcome, no fallback, and no pre-mapped decision tree for distress.
  • When conditions shift — asset values drop, revenue compresses, a fixed obligation like a five-year lease cannot be renegotiated — the organization has no pre-authorized response. Leadership improvises under pressure, which is the worst time to be making structural decisions.
  • What he puts in its place is a mandatory set of plans labeled B, C, and D, each built around a specific adverse scenario, with explicit answers to three questions before any commitment is signed: how far could the asset fall, how long could the distressed position be held, and what refinancing options exist at that point.
  • He stress-tests asset positions against a 40% decline scenario specifically — not because 40% is guaranteed, but because loan covenants often require covering the gap between reduced asset value and the outstanding loan balance, and almost nobody models that obligation in advance.
  • The lesson he draws is that 80% of planning effort should go toward loss mitigation, not toward optimizing the upside — because the upside largely takes care of itself, while the downside is what ends companies.
07 · Worth Knowing
You described the global financial system as running on a cycle that is predictable and repeatable — how does that cycle actually work, and who controls the timing of it?
  • Dmitri's core structural argument is that financial cycles are not random market events — they function like an irrigation system with a controlled tap. Money flows in, assets bloom, and the people controlling the tap know exactly when the supply will be cut because they are the ones cutting it.
  • When the tap closes, asset prices fall and debt obligations do not — a borrower who had $10 million in assets against $8 million in debt can find themselves underwater on paper when assets compress to $6 million, triggering covenant clauses that require covering the gap immediately, not at the bottom of the cycle.
  • The people controlling the tap are positioned in advance: they hold cash or short positions during the compression phase, then buy assets at the bottom for a fraction of their prior value — the same mechanism Dmitri says repeated in 2008 and that he predicts will repeat around 2028.
  • The compound interest dynamic amplifies this across generations: Dmitri's illustration is a single coin placed at 5% interest at the time of Christ, which would by now represent more gold than exists on earth — meaning the structural advantage of being on the receiving end of compounding interest is effectively permanent and self-reinforcing.
  • His conclusion is that the cycle cannot be beaten from inside it — the only available response for ordinary people is to reduce fixed obligations before the next compression, hold assets that are less susceptible to coordinated price manipulation, and map out refinancing options before they are needed rather than after asset values have already fallen.
