Episode 488 · The Misfit Entrepreneur

Found Money: Turn Dormant Connections Into Real Revenue with Anthony Simonie

with Anthony Simonie — Co-Founder, Found Money Agency

Apple Podcasts Spotify YouTube
The 60-Second Version
  • Anthony traced his entrepreneurial drive to his grandmother's relentless work ethic and a pivotal moment of betrayal by a business partner that forced him to find deeper purpose beyond money.
  • He argues that AI and marketing automation have created more noise but less trust, making genuine human-to-human relationship-building a competitive advantage, especially with high-net-worth audiences.
  • His LinkedIn framework starts with profile optimization—cover image, photo, headline, and about section—because prospects always check your profile before they engage, and it functions as a more trusted asset than your own website.
  • He outlined a 'bridge the gap' process that moves LinkedIn connections into an owned email database without damaging domain reputation, enabling multi-channel follow-up that compounds over time.
  • His Misfit Three: take action now instead of waiting for perfect conditions, actively help your neighbors and community, and say what needs to be said—including forgiving those who wronged you—so you carry as little regret as possible.
About the Guest

Anthony Simonie

Anthony Simonie is a serial entrepreneur with over 25 years in marketing, sales, and business development. As co-founder of Found Money Agency and self-titled Chief Giving Officer, he helps entrepreneurs, financial advisors, and business owners attract high-net-worth investors and referral partners through strategic LinkedIn campaigns, email systems, and webinars. His work focuses on activating the relationships and connections businesses already have but have never learned to monetize.

The Conversation

What We Covered

01 · The Origin Story
You have a 25-plus year career spanning a lot of different areas. Take five minutes or so and tell us how you got to where you are today.
  • Anthony was raised by his Italian grandmother, who drilled into him that nothing great comes easy and no one will hand you anything — a philosophy he draws on constantly.
  • He tried to become a professional baseball player in college, then chased that same competitive rush in sales, first as a pharmaceutical rep and then in medical devices at Johnson & Johnson.
  • Feeling capped and undervalued at J&J — where a record-breaking year just meant a higher bar and lower effective pay the next year — he left when a friend described making roughly a thousand dollars a day cleaning out foreclosed properties.
  • He built that property-services business across Missouri, California, and Arizona in 2008 and 2009, put two brothers in charge of two states, and then co-founded a coaching business teaching people in financial distress how to survive the downturn.
  • A business partner exploited a family emergency — Anthony's nine-month-old son nearly died and required CPR, and while Anthony was in the hospital, the partner had him sign documents that transferred half of Anthony's equity under false pretenses.
  • Reading testimonials from clients who saved their marriages and kept their homes during the financial crisis shifted Anthony's entire motivation — the feeling of changing someone's life replaced the pursuit of money as the engine that drives him.
02 · The Breakthrough
You specialize in helping people reach high-net-worth investors and referral partners. What do most businesses get wrong when they try to attract that kind of person today?
  • The core mistake is sounding like you have an ulterior motive the moment you make contact — high-net-worth individuals know everyone wants their money, so any whiff of a pitch ends the relationship immediately.
  • Copying tactics that appear to work for visible operators like Alex Hormozi is a trap: those operators run thousands of ad creative variations and the person copying has no idea which variable is actually producing results.
  • In markets like real estate syndication, competing on promised returns turns into a shouting match — sophisticated investors want stable, predictable performance, not the highest number on a flyer.
  • The only thing that consistently works with affluent prospects is being real — no clever hooks, no borrowed frameworks, just a conversation that sounds like two people meeting somewhere ordinary.
  • Anthony's own client relationship with Dave began as a genuine conversation about shared values and synergies, not a pitch — that origin is the model he teaches.
03 · The Growth Engine
Walk us through your approach — you've described it as attract, convert, retain. Take us through how you actually execute that on LinkedIn and what breaks down for most businesses.
  • The starting point is LinkedIn profile optimization: cover image, profile photo that actually looks like you today, a headline written for the audience you want rather than just your title, and an about section structured like a sales letter with white space and short punchy sentences.
  • LinkedIn is preferred by search engines and increasingly cited by AI tools, so a neglected profile is actively working against you every time a prospect looks you up — the profile is either an asset or a liability whether you tend to it or not.
  • Posting structured content that covers the problems you solve, why people trust you, and who you are warms the algorithm and keeps you visible to existing connections, but messaging behind the scenes is what actually moves revenue.
  • The proprietary move Anthony calls bridge the gap: downloading LinkedIn connection data, then re-engaging those contacts over email in a carefully sequenced way that protects domain reputation and email sending scores — because damaged sender scores can silently kill revenue for months.
  • Once contacts are in a owned database, the channel mix expands to email, direct mail, text, and phone calls, giving the business control that a rented platform like LinkedIn can never provide.
  • Referral partners are activated by sending them genuinely useful content — webinars and guides focused on the partner's business rather than asking for referrals — which creates what Anthony calls the Oprah effect: the partner who shares the content gets the credit without having to be the expert, creating reciprocity that generates introductions.
04 · The Filter
If someone is listening and wants a simple place to start on LinkedIn today, what would you tell them to do first — and what broader marketing move would make the biggest difference right now?
  • Anthony passes on the spray-and-pray numbers game entirely: the business is built on fewer, higher-quality connections with high-net-worth individuals whose lifetime referral value far exceeds what volume-based outreach can produce.
  • He declines to chase the other major social platforms as primary channels, treating LinkedIn as the one platform where the audience's perception of legitimacy is high enough to justify the investment — other platforms are supplementary, not core.
  • Any marketing tactic that sounds clever but doesn't build a relationship is rejected, regardless of how well it appears to work for someone else, because the first bad impression with an affluent prospect is permanent.
  • On the email side, the filter is domain health: if a campaign risks blacklisting the sender domain, it doesn't run — protecting a long-held branded domain is treated as a non-negotiable constraint that overrides short-term send volume.
  • Selling into markets that compete purely on yield — like some corners of the multifamily syndication space — is territory Anthony avoids, because the shouting match dynamic makes relationship-based positioning nearly impossible to sustain.
05 · The People
You've described yourself as someone driven by watching people win. How does that actually show up in how you run your business and serve clients?
  • Anthony's operating philosophy is stated directly on his LinkedIn profile: people and results are the two things that matter most — not as a tagline but as the actual filter he applies to every decision.
  • He gives away substantial content — webinars, guides, deep tactical material — without requiring anyone to become a client, because he believes if someone leaves having wasted their time, that relationship is permanently closed, while genuine value creates an open door forever.
  • His title, Chief Giving Officer, was given to him by others who watched him speak and deliver at events, not something he chose for himself — it reflects how clients and audiences actually experience working with him.
  • He measures his own days against a specific question he asks himself each night: did I serve people the best I could today, and did I make someone else's life better — a standard he holds openly in front of his kids.
  • The shift from chasing revenue to chasing impact happened when client testimonials about saved marriages and preserved homes during the 2008 crisis hit him harder than any commission check ever had, and that fuel has not changed.
06 · What Broke
You've worked with businesses whose revenue dropped year over year and they couldn't figure out why. What was actually broken?
  • The specific thing that breaks is email domain reputation — a technical layer most business owners never monitor until revenue has already been quietly bleeding for months.
  • When Anthony's team audits a struggling client's email ecosystem, they routinely find the business on spam blacklists it never knew existed, or a misconfigured technical setting that silently rerouted outbound messages away from inboxes.
  • The failure mode is almost always triggered by a well-intentioned but unskilled bulk send — downloading LinkedIn connections and blasting them without a warm-up sequence damages the sender score in ways that can take months or longer to repair.
  • What replaced the broken approach is the process Anthony calls bridge the gap: a carefully sequenced re-engagement campaign that moves LinkedIn contacts to email in stages, with deliberate send volumes designed to protect domain health rather than maximize short-term reach.
  • A branded domain held for years is treated as a non-negotiable asset — the lesson is that protecting it ranks above any single campaign's send volume, because the cost of rebuilding a blacklisted domain far exceeds the cost of going slower.
07 · Worth Knowing
Why does LinkedIn specifically outperform other platforms when you're trying to reach high-net-worth prospects, even if those prospects are also active elsewhere?
  • The advantage is perceptual, not algorithmic: high-net-worth individuals treat LinkedIn as a regulated or credentialed environment, which means they extend a baseline level of trust to profiles there that they do not extend to the same person's Facebook or Instagram presence.
  • This perception gap means LinkedIn is doing trust-building work passively — a well-optimized profile encountered during a prospect's due diligence carries more weight than almost any outbound message the business could send.
  • Search engines already favor LinkedIn pages in their rankings, and AI tools are increasingly pulling from LinkedIn when summarizing who someone is — so a neglected profile is actively undermining a business every time a prospect or lender runs a search, whether the owner knows it or not.
  • Other platforms are not wrong choices, but they are supplementary — Anthony's framework treats LinkedIn as the one platform where the audience's perception of legitimacy justifies making it the primary trust-building channel, with everything else feeding into or off of that foundation.
  • The platform's credibility cannot be replicated by spending more on ads elsewhere — it is a structural feature of how that specific audience reads professional signals, which is why businesses that abandon LinkedIn because of spam frustration are giving up the one channel where their target buyer is already primed to believe them.

The Misfit 3™

Anthony's Three

The three things Anthony would leave behind for the generations that come after.

01

Take Action Now

None of us know what happens later today or tomorrow. Taking action applies to your business and your family—sometimes the right action is pushing pause and going to hug your kids. The action takers are the ones who do the best, even when they lose sometimes.

02

Help Your Neighbor

We never know what someone is going through, and they're just not showing it. A simple 'is there any way I can help you today?' can make a real difference in someone's life. Those moments embed something in your kids that no lesson can teach.

03

Say What You Need to Say

Say it when you need to say it, because we never know when our number gets punched. If someone did you wrong and you're holding onto it, the only person hurting is you. Learn to forgive, find the silver lining, and go out with the least amount of regret you can.

Put It To Work

Five Things You Can Act On

  1. Treat Your LinkedIn Profile Like a Sales Letter.Your profile is being looked at whether you manage it or not—by prospects, referral partners, and lenders. Optimize four areas first: cover image, profile photo, headline, and about section. Is your LinkedIn profile working for you or against you right now?
  2. Move Your LinkedIn Connections Into Your Own Database.You are renting LinkedIn but you own your email list. Download your connections and bridge the gap with a warm, story-driven email sequence—not a mass blast. When LinkedIn changes its rules or an account gets restricted, do you have a way to still reach your network?
  3. Stop Treating Referral Partnerships Like a Passive Strategy.Your referral partners are not thinking about you—they are thinking about their own business and family. Build an active process to stay in front of them and bring them value, like sharing content that helps their business, not just yours. When did you last do something for a referral partner that had nothing to do with getting a referral back?
  4. Drop the Pitch and Lead With Relationship on Every Platform.High-net-worth individuals know everyone wants their money, so the moment you sound like you have an ulterior motive they are gone. Talk to people online the same way you would if you ran into them at the grocery store. If you read your last three outreach messages out loud, would they sound like a friend or a carnival barker?
  5. Use AI and Technology to Enhance Human Connection, Not Replace It.People are becoming more resistant to AI-driven communication and want to know you are real and that you care. The businesses winning right now are the ones using technology as a tool while keeping the human relationship at the center. What would your prospects say if you asked them whether they feel like they know you or just your automation?
